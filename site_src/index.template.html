  1. Question, entity, target date

    As of October 4, 2026, who is the legal owner of The Washington Post, what is the chain up to the ultimate parent, who controls it, and who is the publisher and CEO?

    Same wording, same date in the instructions, same output schema for every configuration.

  2. Documents obtained

    The bounded workflow ran 3 fixed searches and kept 15 documents. Only one sentence in them names WP Company LLC: the 2013 purchase agreement (dated August 5, 2013), which lists it among the seller's subsidiaries, before the sale. The brand is tied to Nash Holdings (an encyclopedia infobox, a 2013 news story); the company that publishes it is not.

    Native search ran 3 searches in each of its three passes.

  4. First point where the error is observable

    In the dossier, before any model reads it: no retrieved document dated after 2013 names the legal entity behind the paper. From there the two models part ways. One declines to answer; the other promotes the parent to owner and cites 2013 coverage of the sale.

  5. Interventions tested

    Three, one at a time. A different model on the identical dossier. An extraction step told to follow the chain upward, on the identical documents. And native search instead of the workflow.

  6. Result

    Changing the model named the parent as legal owner instead of leaving a blank. Changing the extraction changed nothing: still blank 3 of 3, still wrong 3 of 3. Native search returned WP Company LLC every time, citing an amicus brief filed October 6, 2025; the passage is in the document:

    "WP Company LLC d/b/a The Washington Post is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Nash Holdings LLC"

  7. What was not corrected

    The workflow's searches still never ask for the publishing entity, so the gap stays. Native search is a whole recipe, not one setting, so this run cannot say which part of it found the brief: cause not observable. Our own record leans on 2013 filings for this link; no public filing since then documents Nash Holdings' ownership.

What we would change, and what this run does not show

Each change below rests on what the runs show. None was tested as a fix here; the last line of each says what to test first.

    What this bench does not show

    • Accuracy in production. Eight cases are enough to see failure modes, not to rank providers.
    • The consumer apps. The same questions in ChatGPT and the Gemini app, on four cases picked before the run (Washington Post, WFAA, KRZZ, KPFA), have not been run yet.
    • Batch, concurrency and retries. Calls ran up to eight at a time; none timed out and none needed a retry.
    • Gemini's own search. Google's terms bar analyzing or redistributing grounded results, so Gemini is tested only on reading the workflow's documents.
    • Who checked the record: two separate AI agent passes over public sources, not a person. Four reference decisions came out of the second pass, all taken before any answer was scored; four scorer fixes were made after the first scores were seen. Both are listed in the repository.

    What the run cost

    List prices of October 4, 2026. Every OpenAI web search item (search, open page, find in page) is billed at the search rate, which may overstate it. The runner stops before any call that would take spending past $15; it cannot cap the searches inside one native-search call.
